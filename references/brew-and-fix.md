# Brew, milk, freshness, fixes

## The two knobs
1. **Strength (TDS)** — how concentrated. Controlled mainly by **ratio**. Weak/watery but balanced → more coffee, not a finer grind.
2. **Extraction (yield)** — how completely the grounds were dissolved. Controlled mainly by **grind**, then time, then temp/agitation. Sour/thin/salty → under. Bitter/dry/harsh → over.

Priority when fixing a cup: grind first → then time/temp → then ratio. Change **one** thing.

Sour **and** bitter together usually means uneven extraction / channeling / mixed particle sizes, not "the beans."

The SCA 18–22% extraction / 1.15–1.35% TDS box is a training tool, not a moral law. Home specialty often brews a bit stronger than Golden Cup.

## Ratios and water
- Home filter start: **1:15–1:17** by weight.
- SCA Golden Cup: **55 g/L ±10%** (~**1:18**), TDS 1.15–1.35%, extraction 18–22%, water **93°C ±3°C**. Those two ratio cultures disagree; both are real.
- Espresso: **dose:yield**, typically **1:2** in ~25–35 s.
- Weigh grams, not scoops.
- Water is ~99% of the cup. Filtered tap is the beginner move. Chlorine ruins flavor. Skip distilled.
- SCAA-shaped water target (orientation): TDS ~75–250 mg/L, some hardness, pH near 7, chlorine 0. High alkalinity flattens brightness.
- Home temp rule: just off boil (~200°F / 93°C). Light roast toward hot; dark roast back off boil. "Hotter = bitter" often just means you extracted more.

## Method → who it's for

| Method | Grind / start ratio / time | Cup | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Espresso | Fine; 1:2 in ~25–35 s | Concentrated, viscous | Milk drinks; intensity. High skill/cost |
| Americano / long black | Espresso + hot water | Espresso flavor, filter volume | Espresso taste without milk |
| Pour-over (V60) | Medium-fine; 1:15–1:17; ~2:30–3:30 | Clean, bright | Flavor nerds; washed Africa/Colombia |
| Chemex | Medium-coarse; 1:16–1:17; ~4–5 min | Extra clean (thick filter) | People who hate sediment/oils |
| Auto drip | Medium; ~1:16–1:18 | Consistent daily | Most households. Temp + fresh grind still matter |
| French press | Coarse; ~1:15; ~4 min | Heavy, oily, textured | "I want body." Easy. Sediment is normal |
| AeroPress | Medium-fine to fine; 1:6–1:17 (no standard) | Forgiving, sweet, travel-proof | Best first "upgrade" brewer. Recipes disagree wildly |
| Moka | Fine-medium; pot-defined, ~1:7–1:12 | Intense, not true espresso | Strong cup, no machine |
| Clever / steep-and-release | Medium; 1:15–1:16 | Immersion + paper clarity | Pour-over flavor, French-press ease |
| Cold brew | Coarse; concentrate often 1:4–1:8, 12–24 h, then dilute | Smooth, low perceived acid | Iced; people who find hot coffee harsh |
| Pods | Fixed | Convenient, usually flatter | Valid. Upgrade = better pods or a bag + drip |

## Milk vs black
- Milk adds sweetness and body and buffers acidity. People who "don't like coffee" often like coffee+milk. That is not a failure.
- Steam to about **55–65°C**, hard max **~70°C**. Above that: cooked flavor, collapsed foam, less perceived sweetness.
- Whole milk foams richest; oat is the usual alt that steams well (brand-dependent); almond scorches easily.
- **Milk hides origin.** Delicate washed Ethiopia disappears in a 12-oz latte. Pair milk with chocolate/nut medium or espresso blends (Brazil, Latin blends). Save floral/fruit lots for black filter.
- Drink map (café convention): espresso → macchiato → cortado / flat white → cappuccino → latte → mocha. Americano = espresso + water.

## Freshness, storage, buying
- Buy **roast date**, not a 2-year "best by."
- Day-0 is often worse (CO₂ blocks even wetting). Sources disagree on the exact rest window; they agree brand-new is often uneven. Many bags open around 5–14 days; some dense light roasts later.
- Buy as much as you'll drink in 2–3 weeks. Use an opened bag within ~2 weeks.
- Store **whole bean**, airtight, cool / dry / dark. Grind at brew time.
- **Fridge: don't.** Coffee is porous and picks up odors.
- **Freezer:** experts disagree. Fine for portioned excess you will not finish in two weeks (airtight, don't refreeze). Pointless for a normal bag you'll finish.
- Whole bean > pre-ground.

## Problem → fix

Separate strength from extraction. Weak ≠ under-extracted.

| They say | Usually means | First fix | Then |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bitter, harsh, dry, ashy | Over-extracted (or stale / too-dark) | Grind **coarser** | Shorter time; slightly cooler water; check roast date |
| Sour, sharp, salty, thin, "unripe" | Under-extracted | Grind **finer** | Hotter water; longer contact; full saturation/bloom |
| Weak, watery, but tastes "fine" | Too little coffee (low TDS) | **More coffee** (1:17 → 1:15) | Don't grind finer yet |
| Sour + bitter | Uneven extraction / channeling / blade grinder | Level the bed; better grind; even pour | Fresh beans; clean gear |
| Flat, dull, cardboard | Stale or faded | New bag with a roast date | Airtight storage |
| Muddy / sludgy | Grind too fine for immersion, or pressed hard | Coarser; paper filter | — |
| "Too acidic" but they mean fruit-bright | Preference, not a brew fail | Switch to Brazil/Colombia medium, or add milk | Don't "fix" a washed Kenya by over-extracting it |
| Gassy, bitter espresso, day 1 | Too soon off roast | Rest 5–14+ days | — |

Acidity ≠ sour. Crisp/winey acidity can be a feature; sour is usually under-extraction or a defect. Perceived sourness tracks titratable acidity / TDS more than pH.
