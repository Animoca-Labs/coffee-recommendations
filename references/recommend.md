# How to recommend

## Voice
- Warm, specific, never snobby. Never "you've been drinking bad coffee."
- One insight per reply. One recommendation, one next step.
- Separate description from liking. "This often tastes like blackcurrant" is not "you should like it."
- If they say sour, believe them. Do not lecture that "it's acidity, actually."

## Ask only what you still need (max 8)
1. Milk or black (or milk/cream/sugar)?
2. What do they like in food/drinks: chocolate / nut / caramel vs fruit / citrus vs "I hate bitter" vs "I hate sour"?
3. How do they brew now, and what gear do they have?
4. Heavier/stronger, or lighter/cleaner?
5. Hot or iced?
6. How much fuss (one button vs a 4-minute ritual)?
7. Anything they already dislike (sour, bitter, watery, "like tea")?
8. Decaf / caffeine limits, only if it matters.

Stop as soon as you can recommend.

## Then decide

| If they… | Steer toward | Avoid as a first step |
|---|---|---|
| Drink milk / hate bitterness | Brazil or Colombia, medium, chocolate-nut; latte/flat white or moka/espresso blend | Delicate washed Ethiopia as a first black cup |
| Drink black, like chocolate/nutty/"normal coffee" | Brazil, Colombia, Guatemala, Peru; medium; drip or French press | Ultra-light competition lots |
| Like fruit / wine / juice / "bright" | Ethiopia natural (jammy berry) or washed (tea, citrus, floral); Kenya if they like punchy acidity | Dark roast that will bury fruit |
| Say "strong" | First ask: concentrated, or roasty/bitter? Tighter ratio (1:15) or espresso/moka for concentrated; darker only if they mean roast flavor | Assuming dark roast = more caffeine |
| Say "not bitter" | Fresher beans + coarser grind or slightly cooler/shorter brew; chocolatey medium roast | Jumping to expensive espresso gear |
| Say "not sour / not like tea" | Medium Latin America or Brazil; immersion (French press / Clever) | A light washed Africa first |
| Have no gear | One bag of fresh whole-bean medium + cheapest burr grinder, or a café pour-over of a chocolatey Colombia | Pour-over theater |
| Only have a pod/drip machine | Better beans (or a better pod) + a 1:16–1:17 weigh-up if they can | Buying three new brewers |

When a fix and a shopping list are both possible, fix it with the gear they already have.

## Everyday likes → bag

| They like | Beans | Roast | Brew |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chocolate, nutty, caramel | Brazil, Colombia, Guatemala, Peru | Medium | Drip, press, espresso + milk |
| Fruit, jam, wine | Ethiopia natural; some Kenyas; honeys | Light–medium | Pour-over, AeroPress, black |
| Bright, citrus, tea | Ethiopia / Kenya / Rwanda washed | Light–medium | Pour-over, Chemex |
| Sweet (no syrup) | Naturals, honeys, milk at 55–65°C, medium roast | Medium | Latte / honey process / not burnt milk |
| Not bitter | Fresh medium roast; coarser; don't boil dark roast | Medium | Press or drip; milk OK |
| Not sour | Brazil / Latin medium; immersion | Medium | Press, Clever, milk |

## Translate

| Pro word | Human |
|---|---|
| Acidity | Brightness — apple/citrus sparkle, not spoiled-milk sour |
| Body | How heavy it feels (skim vs whole milk) |
| Clarity | Can you pick flavors apart, or is it one blob |
| Extraction | How much flavor the water pulled out |
| Strength / TDS | How concentrated the cup is |
| Origin character | Tastes like the bean/place, not just toast |
| Funky | Winey / fermented-fruit; some love it |
| Mouthfeel | Texture, not flavor |

## Beginner insights (pick one per chat)
1. Strong and extracted are different. Ratio = strength; grind = extraction.
2. "Honey process" has no honey. Wet-hulled Sumatra is also not washed.
3. Darker is not stronger and not more caffeinated. It's roast flavor.
4. Roast date beats the best-by stamp — and day-1 isn't the peak. CO₂ makes brand-new beans brew uneven. Many bags are better at day 5–14.
5. If it's bitter, grind coarser; if it's sour, grind finer. One change. Water is almost the whole drink; chlorine is a silent killer.

Optional sixth: milk steamed past ~70°C tastes cooked, not "extra hot and better."
